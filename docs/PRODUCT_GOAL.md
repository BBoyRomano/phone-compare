# Product Goal

## Mission

Build, operate, and continuously improve a public web application that helps people accurately compare mobile phones.

The product should remain useful over time without requiring routine product, design, engineering, or data-management decisions from the repository owner.

## Core User Outcome

A user should be able to understand meaningful differences between phones and compare them using accurate, well-structured, and clearly sourced information.

The application should make complex device specifications understandable without sacrificing factual accuracy.

## Product Coverage

The product should maintain relevant mobile phone models throughout their lifecycle.

New phones should be incorporated when they become officially available or are officially announced to an extent that makes inclusion useful.

Phones that are no longer considered part of the actively sold product catalogue should remain accessible as historical or archived products when their information remains useful for comparison or reference.

The exact lifecycle definitions and mechanisms used to determine these states may evolve as the product matures.

## Data Quality

Product specifications, launch information, and original pricing should be derived from authoritative primary sources whenever reasonably possible.

Manufacturer websites, official documentation, official announcements, regulatory documentation, and other first-party materials should be preferred over secondary aggregators.

Every factual product datum should be traceable to sufficient provenance to allow its origin and reliability to be evaluated later.

Information must not be silently invented, inferred as fact without appropriate qualification, or presented with unjustified certainty.

When reliable sources disagree, disappear, are incomplete, or vary by market, the product should preserve that uncertainty rather than conceal it.

## Original Pricing

Where available, the product should preserve the original official launch or recommended retail price together with enough context to interpret it correctly.

Relevant context may include currency, market or region, configuration, launch date, and source.

Current retailer pricing is not a substitute for original official pricing.

## Product Autonomy

The product may evolve substantially over time.

Its implementation may independently determine and revise:

- product features;
- information architecture;
- user experience;
- visual design;
- technical architecture;
- data models;
- data acquisition methods;
- search and comparison mechanisms;
- correction and feedback mechanisms;
- maintenance processes;
- prioritization methods;
- external contribution workflows.

No particular implementation, framework, interface pattern, or internal architecture is part of the product goal.

## External Input

Feedback, corrections, feature requests, issues, and suggestions from users or external contributors may be considered as product input.

Such input is not automatically authoritative and does not create an obligation to implement a requested change.

The product should evaluate external input according to evidence, user value, reliability, product alignment, complexity, maintenance burden, and other relevant considerations.

## Long-Term Quality

The product should continuously optimize for:

- factual reliability;
- usefulness;
- clarity;
- maintainability;
- accessibility;
- performance;
- resilience;
- appropriate operating cost.

Short-term implementation convenience should not unnecessarily compromise long-term product quality.

## Success

The product is successful when users can reliably use it to understand and compare phones, while the underlying system can continue discovering, verifying, maintaining, and improving its information and functionality with minimal routine intervention from the repository owner.
